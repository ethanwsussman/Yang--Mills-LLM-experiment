# YMM1 — revision of 26 October 2026

This folder contains the revised manuscript in `YMM1-2026-10-26.tex` and `YMM1-2026-10-26.pdf`. The current manuscript is published as [YM3_I.tex](../YM3_I.tex) and [YM3_I.pdf](../YM3_I.pdf).

The original supplied text (“slop”) contains the hypotheses and proof steps listed below. Its corresponding arguments are correct under those hypotheses. The changes restore that content in YMM1; they do not introduce replacement constructions. This statement concerns the arguments listed here, not every passage of the supplied text.

Page references below are to the preceding 854-page edition of YMM1. The revised edition may have different pagination. The statements of the principal Theorems 0 and A–C are unchanged.

1. **Abstract (p. 1).** State the admitted torus family, correlation orders of at least two, and empty common intersection of test supports. State the canonical first-passage and small-reference-coupling hypotheses for the Gibbs/component conclusions, and the further coupling restriction for the short-distance law. The original arguments have these hypotheses, as do the principal theorem statements in YMM1.

2. **Smooth cutoffs (p. 182).** Specify translated and rescaled copies of one fixed smooth product cutoff and their uniform scaled Lipschitz bound. The original Part I proof makes this choice before estimating the partition; the revision restores it.

3. **Global and local fluctuation sets (p. 301).** Require global membership before concluding local membership, and express the site conclusion in the site index set. The original Part I comparison proves the statement with these premises. Proximity is used together with global membership in that proof and in the cutoff application.

4. **Enclosing intervals and cubes (pp. 378, 482).** State that each selected interval lies in a larger parent interval meeting the original interval at a bare coordinate. Use these parent cubes in the subsequent normalizer application. The original Part II proof establishes this parent-interval property and uses it to obtain cube containment; it does not require every selected child interval to meet the original interval.

5. **Analytic strip estimates (pp. 447–448 and subsequent uses).** State the bounded-increment and running-coupling conditions before the analytic strip assertions, including the large-log and smallness conditions. Display their consequence $\varepsilon_k<\varepsilon_t\le\sqrt{2L}\,\varepsilon_k$, and identify the comparison used for strip regularity. The original Part II proof works in this regime and proves the comparison. The canonical trajectories satisfy the stated conditions.

6. **Majorant hypotheses (pp. 581–582).** Include $|b_i|\le B_\sharp$ for the whole prefix in Proposition 65.4 and write out the downward induction using $x_{i-1}=L(x_i+b_i)$. The original Part II argument uses the bounded increments to obtain the monotonicity and smallness conclusions needed in this proof.

7. **Turns at stencil sites (p. 521).** Restrict the assertion about neighboring tops to sites with nonzero turn. The original Part II proof makes precisely this restriction. Sites with zero turn contribute zero; the stated numerical bounds are retained.

8. **Wilson members and action sums (pp. 537–539, 632).** Restrict the plaquette sums and their weighted version to inner bare plaquettes, whose four corners lie in the region. Specify this restriction in the curvature estimate and the partition used for the action identity. The original Parts II and III definitions and proofs already sum over these inner plaquettes, so all bonds used in the estimate lie in the controlled region.

9. **Joint analytic radius (earlier radius lemma and p. 580).** Include the relative two-input averaging logarithms in the common choice of $r_*$ in Lemma 14.7. State their joint holomorphy and bound, and include products, inverses and logarithms in the finite-family radius argument. Lemma 65.3 then uses this same joint radius. The original Part I radius proof includes the relative maps, and the original Part II pivot calculation identifies the map used here. The formulas for the derivative bound and the ensuing cutoff remain unchanged.

10. **Canonical centered member (pp. 634, 640).** State its admitted triple and two Taylor-domain bounds. Require the mean-shifted field bound when identifying the positive-source remainder with that member, and check definedness before cancellation. The original Part III argument checks these domains before adding the analytic terms. The polynomial positive-source identity keeps its existing, broader domain.

11. **Coarsening constant (p. 551).** Define $C'_{\rm col}=13/9$ and cite the coarsening estimate that proves it. The original Part II estimate supplies this coefficient; the revision changes the definition and reference at its later use.

12. **Flatness cutoff notation (p. 592 and earlier uses).** Define $\varepsilon_\flat=c^{\mathrm{SO}}_2$ before its first use. The original Part II text defines the alias and verifies the equality. No cutoff value is changed.

13. **Absent-tail convention (p. 634).** State that a term associated with an empty index set is zero, written $e^{-c(+\infty)}=0$ for $c\ge0$, including $c=0$. The original Part III estimate explicitly uses this convention at the endpoint.

14. **Positive source majorant (p. 646).** Require $s_0>0$ in the quadratic-weight lemma. The original Part III proof assumes a positive majorant when defining its constants. A zero source is still admitted with any positive majorant.

15. **Full fluctuation box (p. 658).** Define the full real box for the internal and external bond variables in the Borel-domain statement. The original Parts II and III distinguish this box from the internal integration box. The integration range is unchanged.

16. **Cube size in a kept term (p. 659).** Specify a big cube of side $LM$ in level-$k$ units. The original Part III proof establishes containment of this cube in both the domain and the strip region; it does not require containment of the larger strip cube.

17. **External-mean admission (p. 664 and earlier uses).** Define $\varepsilon_{\rm xt}=\tfrac14(LM)^{-3/2}$ and retain the associated far-term admission inequality in the final coupling choice. Use an inequality when applying the canonical ceiling to the actual mean parameter. The original Parts II and III distinguish those parameters, give this tolerance and use $(LM)^{3/2}\varepsilon_{\rm xt}=1/4$ in the Gaussian estimate.

18. **History weight (p. 673).** Write the product as $f(h)\exp(e(\mathcal S(h)))$. The original Part III argument exponentiates the additive deficit, as do the operative identities already retained in YMM1.

19. **Cauchy weights (p. 704).** Divide the test-function weights by the analytic radius when applying the unit-ball derivative lemma. The original Part IV proof uses these normalized weights. The final derivative estimates already have the corresponding powers of the radius and remain unchanged.

20. **Invariance in the two-state limit (p. 710).** State lattice-translation and signed-permutation invariance for both the integrating and centering laws. The original Part IV proof assumes both invariances when transporting reflection positivity to the other coordinate mirrors. The periodic-Gibbs/component applications have these invariances.

21. **Insertion bonds on the quotient (p. 750).** Require injectivity of the entire indexed union of insertion bonds into the horizontal quotient, including injectivity within each insertion set. The original Part IV proof states and checks this condition before relabeling operators and taking their tensor product.

22. **Boundary-layer count (p. 752).** Use the sup-norm displacements in the boundary definition: a diagonal coordinate can change by two lattice spacings, so two layers at each diagonal end enter the count. The original Part IV proof counts these layers and obtains the numerical bound already printed in YMM1. That bound is unchanged.

23. **Boundary-value growth parameters (p. 771).** State that the real growth parameters $b,p$ are nonnegative. The original Part IV lemma has these hypotheses; they ensure the permitted integration order is positive. The applications use nonnegative parameters.

24. **Moving-label first-passage limit (p. 812).** State explicitly that $K_j\to\infty$, in addition to convergence of the labels. The original Part IV proof invokes the moving-label theorem with this cutoff hypothesis, which is also present in YMM1's referenced theorem.
